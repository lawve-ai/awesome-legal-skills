# COUNTER-AFFIDAVIT IN OPPOSITION TO THE CLAIMANT'S APPLICATION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT

## AFFIDAVIT

I, **[FULL NAME]**, of **[OFFICE ADDRESS OF DEPONENT]**, Lagos State, **[DIRECTOR/AUTHORISED OFFICER]** of the Defendant, make oath and state as follows:

1. I am a [director/an authorised officer] of the Defendant and am conversant with the facts giving rise to this suit. I am duly authorised by the Defendant to depose to this affidavit on its behalf.

2. Except where otherwise stated, the facts deposed to herein are within my knowledge and the exhibits referred to are true copies of the documents maintained by or received by the Defendant in the ordinary course of its business.

3. I have read the Claimant's affidavit in support of the application for summary judgment and the Statement of Claim. I make this affidavit in opposition to the application.

4. The Claimant is correct that the parties entered into a business relationship under which the Claimant provided logistics, courier and stock-movement services to the Defendant.

5. The Defendant does not deny that some invoices were issued or that a balance was outstanding at some point. The Defendant's position is that the Claimant's present claim is overstated because the Claimant is liable for damage caused to the Defendant's goods while those goods were in the Claimant's custody.

6. As at 30 April 2026, the account balance on the Claimant's figures was ₦6,668,906.00. That amount is subject to reconciliation and to the Defendant's claim for set-off.

7. On 31 May 2026, the Defendant paid the sum of ₦2,500,000.00 as the first instalment under the parties' agreed payment arrangement. The payment was received by the Claimant and reduced the account, according to the Claimant's reconciliation, to ₦4,168,906.00.

8. The Defendant's payment and the repayment arrangement were made without abandoning the Defendant's existing concerns concerning products damaged, expired, missing or compromised while in the Claimant's custody. Whether the written repayment arrangement expressly records that reservation is being verified by the Defendant.

9. The Defendant's Nectar Beauty goods were handed to the Claimant's team in good condition and in cartons for carriage to the Defendant's Warri/Delta store.

10. The goods were delivered to the Defendant on or about 2 June 2026. On delivery, the goods had been placed into sacks and were not in the cartons in which they had been handed to the Claimant.

11. On 16 June 2026, the Defendant notified the Claimant, through Ms Sofia Yingilayefa, that the goods had been delivered in a damaged condition and repacked into sacks. The Defendant stated in that correspondence that the value of the damaged goods would be deducted from the outstanding balance. A true copy of the email is exhibited as **Exhibit HD-1**.

12. On 18 June 2026, the Claimant replied and admitted that its team had repackaged the goods into sack bags without notice to or the consent of the Defendant. The Claimant stated that it had sealed the sacks with its own security seal belts and placed them in a hold cage. A true copy of the reply is exhibited as **Exhibit HD-2**.

13. The goods were in the Claimant's custody and control from collection until delivery. It was therefore for the Claimant to take reasonable care of the goods and to preserve their condition during carriage.

14. The Claimant's unauthorised opening and repackaging of the cartons, and the subsequent delivery of damaged goods, constitute prima facie evidence of failure to take reasonable care. The Claimant's admission, made by its own correspondence, is relevant to this issue.

15. The Defendant sent the Claimant photographs and a video showing the condition of the goods. The Defendant's 16 June 2026 email was followed by further transmissions after the Claimant stated that it had not received an earlier video. True copies of the relevant transmission records, photographs and video or their storage records are exhibited as **Exhibits HD-3 to HD-5**.

16. On 19 June 2026, the Defendant provided the Claimant with a schedule listing the damaged products, quantities, unit prices and total values after separating out the saleable items.

17. The schedule showed that the total value of the damaged goods was ₦2,173,116.00. The Defendant avers that this amount is the value of the goods damaged while in the Claimant's custody and that it falls to be set off against any sum found due to the Claimant.

18. The schedule of damaged goods is exhibited as **Exhibit HD-6**. It is being corrected and re-verified by the Defendant before filing. No line or total should be relied upon until the schedule has been reconciled and the basis of valuation confirmed.

19. The net position on the Defendant's pleaded figures is as follows:

| Particular | Amount (₦) |
|---|---:|
| Balance as at 30 April 2026 | 6,668,906.00 |
| Less: payment of 31 May 2026 | (2,500,000.00) |
| Balance according to the Claimant | 4,168,906.00 |
| Less: value of damaged goods | (2,173,116.00) |
| Net sum, if any, due | **1,995,790.00** |

20. The Claimant's affidavit does not establish that its account remained ₦4,168,906.00 despite the Defendant's prior and later claims concerning damaged goods. The Defendant's set-off raises a triable factual issue concerning custody, damage, causation and valuation.

21. The Defendant also disputes the Claimant's entitlement to summary judgment for general damages of ₦1,000,000.00, interest at 10% per annum and costs of ₦500,000.00. The Claimant has not shown any contractual basis for the interest, any particulars of recoverable general loss or any entitlement to solicitor's costs in this Small Claims proceeding.

22. The Defendant denies that a signed waybill or proof of delivery bars the claim. The goods were delivered inside sealed sacks and could not be inspected item by item on receipt. The damage was latent and was discovered when the sacks were opened.

23. The Defendant further denies that a contractual notice period, if applicable, bars the claim. The Defendant notified the Claimant promptly after discovery and gave actual notice. The Claimant cannot rely on a notice requirement to conceal damage that its own unauthorised repackaging made undiscoverable at delivery.

24. **[Confirm whether the parties agreed that the payment arrangement remained subject to reconciliation and whether the Defendant paid the ₦1,000,000.00 instalment due on 31 July 2026.]**

25. Wherefore, I respectfully urge the Honourable Court to:

   - (a) refuse the Claimant's application for summary judgment;
   - (b) permit the Defendant to proceed to trial or other determination of the dispute;
   - (c) recognise or assess the Defendant's set-off of ₦2,173,116.00 against any sum found due to the Claimant;
   - (d) assess only the net sum, if any, properly due after set-off; and
   - (e) make such further or other order as the Court considers just.
