# DEFENDANT'S STATEMENT OF DEFENCE

## A. Introduction

1. The Defendant is a company engaged in pharmacy retail and distribution.

2. Save as expressly admitted, the Defendant denies each allegation in the Claimant's Statement of Claim as if the same were specifically pleaded and traversed.

3. The Defendant admits that it engaged the Claimant to provide logistics, courier and stock-movement services. The Defendant also admits that an account balance was outstanding at a relevant stage. The Defendant's case is that the sum claimed is overstated because the Claimant is liable for goods damaged while in the Claimant's custody, and the value of those goods falls to be set off against any sum properly due.

## B. The Services and Invoices

4. The Defendant admits that the Claimant provided services to the Defendant during the period stated in the Claim. The Defendant nevertheless requires the Claimant to prove each of the following invoices, including the services rendered, applicable rates and delivery records:

   - INV-0265;
   - INV-0416;
   - INV-0420;
   - INV-0422;
   - INV-0423; and
   - INV-0443.

5. The Defendant admits that, as at 30 April 2026, the balance on the account stood at ₦6,668,906.00 according to the Claimant's figures. That amount remains subject to reconciliation and to the set-off pleaded below.

## C. The Payment of ₦2,500,000.00

6. The Defendant admits that it paid ₦2,500,000.00 on 31 May 2026 under the parties' agreed payment arrangement. On the Claimant's figures, the payment reduced the stated account balance to ₦4,168,906.00.

7. The Defendant avers that the payment arrangement was made on the understanding that the account remained subject to reconciliation, including the Defendant's prior concerns regarding goods damaged, expired, missing or compromised while in the Claimant's custody.

8. The Defendant avers that the Claimant released goods held in the Claimant's warehouse only after receipt of the first tranche of ₦2,500,000.00.

## D. Damage to the Defendant's Goods

9. The goods subsequently released to the Defendant were Nectar Beauty products delivered to the Defendant's Warri/Delta store on or about 2 June 2026.

10. The Defendant's goods were handed over to the Claimant's team in good condition and in cartons.

11. By email dated 16 June 2026, the Defendant notified the Claimant, through Ms Sofia Yingilayefa, that the goods had been delivered in a damaged state and repacked into sacks. The Defendant also notified the Claimant that the value of the damaged items would be deducted from the outstanding account balance.

12. By email dated 18 June 2026, the Claimant admitted that its team had repackaged the goods into sack bags. It did so without the prior notice or consent of the Defendant. The Claimant further stated that it had sealed the sacks with its own security seal belts and placed them in a hold cage.

13. The Defendant avers that:

   - (a) the goods were in the Claimant's sole custody and control from collection until delivery;
   - (b) the Claimant opened the Defendant's cartons and repackaged the goods without the Defendant's knowledge or authority;
   - (c) the Claimant owed the Defendant a duty to take reasonable care of the goods in custody and during carriage; and
   - (d) the Claimant breached that duty and caused damage to the goods.

14. On 19 June 2026, the Defendant sent the Claimant a schedule separating the saleable items from the damaged items. The schedule identified the damaged products, quantities, unit prices and total values. The total value of the damaged goods was ₦2,173,116.00.

## E. Response to Anticipated Objections

15. Delivery acknowledgments and waybills do not waive the Defendant's claim. The goods were delivered in sealed sacks and could not, at receipt, be inspected item by item. The damage was latent and was discovered only after the sacks were opened.

16. The Defendant denies that a contractual 48-hour notice period bars this claim. In the alternative, the Defendant avers that it notified the Claimant promptly after discovery of the damage and that the Claimant had actual notice. The Claimant is not entitled to rely on a notice requirement where its own unauthorised repackaging concealed the damage.

17. The Defendant sent the Claimant photographs and a video of the damaged goods after being advised that the Claimant had not received an earlier video transmission.

## F. Set-off and Net Balance

18. The Defendant pleads set-off. The sum of ₦2,173,116.00 due in respect of the damaged goods is to be deducted from the sum found due to the Claimant:

| | Amount (₦) |
|---|---:|
| Balance as at 30 April 2026 | 6,668,906.00 |
| Less: payment of 31 May 2026 | (2,500,000.00) |
| Balance according to the Claimant | 4,168,906.00 |
| Less: value of damaged goods | (2,173,116.00) |
| Net balance, if any, due to the Claimant | **1,995,790.00** |

19. The Claimant's claim for ₦4,168,906.00 is therefore overstated by ₦2,173,116.00.

20. The Defendant denies that it is liable for the full sum claimed. The account is subject to reconciliation and set-off of the proven value of the damaged goods.

## G. Damages, Interest and Costs

21. The Claimant's claim is for a liquidated debt. The Defendant denies that the Claimant has suffered loss beyond that debt that would justify an award of ₦1,000,000.00 in general damages. The Defendant says that the Claimant has pleaded no particulars of any such loss.

22. The Defendant denies that the Claimant is entitled to interest at 10% per annum from 1 August 2026. The Claimant has neither pleaded nor identified any contractual provision entitling it to such interest. In any event, interest, if available, should be assessed only on the net sum, if any, ultimately found due.

23. The Defendant denies that the Claimant is entitled to ₦500,000.00 in costs or solicitor's fees. The Claimant's entitlement to costs is subject to the applicable Small Claims procedure and the Court's discretion.

## H. Counterclaim

24. In the alternative, and only if the Court's rules permit a counterclaim in these proceedings, the Defendant repeats paragraphs 9 to 19 and counterclaims for:

   - (a) set-off of ₦2,173,116.00 against any sum found due to the Claimant;
   - (b) judgment for the difference, if the value of the damaged goods exceeds the sum found due to the Claimant; and
   - (c) such further or other relief as the Court considers appropriate.

## I. Reliefs Sought

The Defendant asks the Court to:

1. dismiss the claim for general damages, interest and costs;
2. find that the sum due to the Claimant, if any, is no more than ₦1,995,790.00 after set-off;
3. grant the pleaded set-off and, if permitted, the Defendant's counterclaim; and
4. make such further or other order as the Court considers fit.
