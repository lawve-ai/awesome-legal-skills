# Beyond the corpus: external sources for foreign and treaty law

When the referential lacks a text (or its currency must be verified), these are the sources that hold up, with their limits. Checked July 2026: re-verify a domain before citing it, some die (below).

## The reflex chain (nationality / filiation / family file)

1. **Is there a France-[country] bilateral convention?** JaFBase first, then the MEAE treaty base; the opposable text is the décret de publication (Légifrance, and often this corpus under INTL).
2. **The foreign substantive law**: national official journal online, Droit-Afrique, Refworld, NATLEX, always pinning the version date.
3. **Lock the proof**: certificat de coutume if needed, knowing the judge must anyway seek the foreign law's content (see foreign-law.md).
4. **Civil-status acts**: article 47 réflexes + légalisation or apostille + underlying foreign judgment when the act executes one.

## Reference sources

- **JaFBase**, `http://www.jafbase.fr` (HTTP only, broken TLS): the practitioners' base for family-law DIP before the French JAF, by a French magistrate, covering bilateral conventions France-country (claims exhaustiveness there) and foreign substantive family law. Its own caveat: foreign statutes not guaranteed still in force.
- **Traités et accords de la France (MEAE)**, `https://basedoc.diplomatie.gouv.fr/pages/traites/`: every treaty France concluded, with signature, entry into force and JORF reference. Formerly « base Pacte » (name dead since 2018). For multilaterals where France is not depositary, indicative only, the depositary (UNTS) is authoritative.
- **Légifrance, droit international**: the décrets de publication, the text as opposable in France.
- **UNTS**, `https://treaties.un.org`: ratification status of multilaterals, kept current.
- **EUR-Lex / N-Lex**, `https://eur-lex.europa.eu`: EU law consolidated (indicative; the JO alone is authentic), gateway to member-state official bases.
- **Portail e-Justice**, `https://e-justice.europa.eu`: per-member-state family-law fact sheets in French, each dated.
- **HUDOC**, `https://hudoc.echr.coe.int`: ECHR case law, EN/FR.
- **Refworld (HCR)**, `https://www.refworld.org`: nationality, statelessness, asylum legislation worldwide. Rebuilt Feb 2024: pre-2024 links may be dead, and country-of-origin information in English moved to `https://www.ecoi.net`.
- **Droit-Afrique**, `https://www.droit-afrique.com`: 24 francophone African countries, 3 000+ free texts, business/tax slant; check each PDF's consolidation date.
- **Juricaf**, `https://juricaf.org`: ~800 000 francophone decisions from 40 countries.
- **Gazettes.Africa**: official journals of ~24 African countries.
- **NATLEX (OIT)** / **WIPO Lex**: worldwide statute notices; often summaries or unofficial English translations, dead outbound links possible.

## Verified pitfalls

- **Dead or hijacked domains**: `juriafrique.com` now serves an Indonesian gambling site. Never cite a remembered URL without checking what it serves today. Use `ohada.com` / `ohada.org` for OHADA law instead.
- **Official but stale**: `jo.gouv.sn` (Senegal's real JO online, texts since 2001) has search gaps; `dri.gouv.sn` is a government documentation site frozen since 2019, with authentic texts but no guarantee of currency. An online JO can lag the paper JO by years.
- **The cited code no longer exists**: the code de la nationalité française was repealed in 1993 (nationality law lives in the Code civil, art. 17 to 33-2) yet still governs declarations subscribed before 1993: ratione temporis, the « dead » code is often the applicable one. Foreign family codes circulate in multiple PDF versions while reforms are pending (Moroccan Moudawana reform in parliament, 2026): say which version, from where, as of when.
- **Unofficial translations**: NATLEX, Refworld, WIPO Lex serve English translations; only the official-language JO text is authentic.
- **Enacted ≠ applied**: international bases record the text as notified; they see neither implicit repeals nor local constitutional case law nor administrative practice.
